## 1. Currency / Exchange Rate Movement (New Syrian Pound)

**Status: No material new movement — baseline holds, with data caveat.**

- TradingEconomics reports USD/SYP at 122.00 on Sept 3, 2026, down 0.02% from the prior session, and flat over the past month (source: tradingeconomics.com/syria/currency, accessed Sept 3, 2026).
- exchange-rates.org shows the new (redenominated) pound trading in a narrow 121.6–122.0 band from Aug 26 through Sept 2, 2026 (Sept 1: 121.73, Sept 2: 121.80) — essentially flat, continuing the post-redenomination stabilization trend, not a new shock (source: exchange-rates.org/exchange-rate-history/usd-syp-2026).
- Forecasting site midforex.com projects continued gentle depreciation (-0.02% to -0.45% over the next month) but no abrupt move; no news of a Central Bank of Syria devaluation or peg change was found for the Sept 1–3 window.
- **Data caveat for the strategy brain**: the historical series shows the redenomination (two zeros dropped) taking effect around Jan 6-8, 2026, after which the rate settled near 110-122 SYP/USD, not the 10,000-11,500 range noted in the existing baseline. Recommend the brain reconcile which figure (pre- vs post-redenomination) is being used in unit-economics models — this is a clarification of the baseline, not a new September event.

## 3. Google Play Console Developer Account Restrictions for Syrian Entities

**Status: No easing found — restriction reconfirmed, still blocked.**

- unblocksyria.com (a community-run status tracker) currently lists Google Play Console as **"restricted"** for Syria. Its timeline shows a February 2026 community report that the console was briefly reachable, followed by a later February 2026 report that new developer account creation fails at the payment step and that Syria is absent from the country-selection dropdown — status was downgraded back to "restricted" and has not been updated since (source: unblocksyria.com/en/services/google-play-console, checked Sept 3, 2026, no dated entries newer than the Feb 2026 reports).
- No Google Play policy update, blog post, or press coverage referencing Syria specifically was found for August or September 2026 (the Play Developer Policy Center's "Latest Information" section shows only generic global policy updates from July 2026, unrelated to country access).
- Note: this only confirms lack of *new* information — it does not rule out an undisclosed/unannounced change; recommend a hands-on test (attempt console sign-up from a Syria-linked account) if this gate is on the launch critical path.

## 6. LEAP 2026 Riyadh — Syria Pavilion (Aug 31–Sep 3) Outcomes

**Status: Confirmed event and details found — sector-level signal, no company-specific announcements captured beyond general framing.**

- Syria participated in **LEAP 2026** (Riyadh International Convention and Exhibition Center, Aug 31–Sep 3, 2026) for the **first time**, with a national pavilion (booth H3.G190) led by Syria's Ministry of Communications and Information Technology (MoCIT), including the Telecommunication and Post Regulatory Authority, the Syrian Sovereign Fund, telecom operators, two tech-community organizations, and **seven Syrian startups** (sources: spa.gov.sa/en/N2664489; sabq.org and saudiarabianewsgazette.com, both dated Aug 30-31, 2026; jarayid.com).
- Minister Abdulsalam Haykal framed the goal as connecting Syrian startups with Saudi/regional venture-capital funds, growing the **"Silk Link" project** (a named Saudi investment in Syrian telecom infrastructure, described as "now underway"), and accelerating digital-transformation/infrastructure programs.
- No dollar-figure MOUs, specific fintech/logistics deal announcements, or concrete regulatory outcomes (e.g., new licensing regime, cross-border payment rail, fintech sandbox) tied to LEAP were found in coverage through Sept 3, 2026 — reporting stays at the level of intent/relationship-building ("explore," "aim to," "seek to") rather than announced deliverables.
- Relevance to the super-app thesis: this confirms continued top-down government push to court foreign (especially Saudi) capital into Syrian digital/telecom infrastructure, and it's the first formal international tech-showcase presence — useful narrative/tailwind context, but no concrete new infrastructure, funding, or regulatory change has landed yet that would directly de-risk a Q4 2026 Latakia launch.
