## 1. Courier/rider monthly income — actual range

🟢 **CONFIRMED (aggregate market range, national):** JobInSyria's 2026 salary survey states delivery drivers/couriers ("سائق توصيل/مندوب") earn **$100–250/month base**, "increasing with tips and commissions." This is presented as a national estimate, not Latakia-specific.
Source: jobinsyria.com, "رواتب المهن في سوريا 2026" (dated 2026, exact publish date not shown, aggregated compensation table) — https://jobinsyria.com/delivery-driver-jobs-syria/ and https://jobinsyria.com/syria-salaries-by-profession-2026/

🟢 **CONFIRMED (structural detail on pay mechanics):** BeeOrder's own driver recruitment site (join-us.beeorder.com) states explicitly:
- **"0% commission on your income"** — BeeOrder claims it does NOT take a cut from courier earnings (implies riders are paid per-delivery from a fee baked into the merchant/customer side, not a revenue-share deducted from rider payouts).
- Minimum activity requirement: **at least 6 hours/day and 21 active days/month** ("so you have 9 rest days/month") to guarantee "steady income."
- Riders are explicitly framed as self-employed/independent contractors, not employees (see §2).
- Onboarding requires a **550,000 SYP refundable equipment/kit deposit** (payable in installments), age 18+, Android phone, clean criminal record, and residency proof.
- Contract termination payout (deposit + earnings) settles within **72 business hours** to the rider's wallet, withdrawable "from any payment center."
Source: https://join-us.beeorder.com/ (live recruitment page, accessed 2026-09-03; BeeOrder operates in Damascus, Aleppo, Homs, **Latakia**, Tartus per its careers page https://careers.beeorder.com/).

🟢 **CONFIRMED (job-posting evidence for Latakia specifically):** Multiple live delivery-rider job posts specifically for Latakia found on jobinsyria.com and Instagram/Facebook (Aug–Sep 2026), e.g.:
- "مندوبو توصيل — شركة توصيل، اللاذقية" (Job In Syria, posted 9 Sept 2026) — https://jobinsyria.com/job/mndwbw-twsyl-shrka-twsyl-alladhqya (no salary figure disclosed in the post itself; apply via phone/WhatsApp).
- "كابتن توصيل (الدراجة مؤمنة) — شركة توربو للتوصيل، طرطوس" — note **"الدراجة مؤمنة"** = "the bike is insured," suggesting at least one competitor in the Latakia/Tartus coastal market advertises rider-bike insurance as a hiring perk (implies it's NOT standard/expected, otherwise wouldn't be a selling point). 🟡 This is a strong signal that motorcycle coverage is inconsistent/optional across operators, reinforcing the "no confirmed universal insurance law" finding.
- "ندهة للتوصيل" (Nadha Delivery) recruiting couriers in Damascus, Tel, Tartus, **Latakia**, Daraa via Instagram (Sept 2026) — https://www.instagram.com/p/DPV9an1iLPK
- Facebook: "فلاش-ديلفري" (Flash Delivery) recruiting 6 couriers in Latakia; requires prior delivery experience — facebook.com/groups/1084540954929642

🔴 **UNCONFIRMED:** No numeric monthly income figure was found specifically stated for Latakia (vs. national aggregate). No pay-per-order rate card (SYP/order) was found publicly for BeeOrder, YallaGo, or Delivery Syria. Real take-home is highly exchange-rate sensitive — SYP was trading ~13,150–13,180/USD as of early Sept 2026 (jobinsyria.com live ticker), so SYP-denominated postings need conversion at time of hiring, not at time of writing.

**Implication for Bosla:** Budget planning should use **$100–250/month** as the *net* range couriers currently earn across gig apps (after fuel is a separate implied cost the rider bears, since BeeOrder's contract explicitly assigns fuel/maintenance/vehicle costs to the rider — see §3). This is consistent with, not contradicting, the earlier $1.20/liter fuel price finding — fuel is a real bite out of that already-thin margin.

## 2. Employment model & labor law status of gig couriers

🟢 **CONFIRMED — couriers are contractors, not employees, at the dominant incumbent (BeeOrder).** The BeeOrder "Terms and Conditions" text couriers must accept on sign-up is explicit and directly quotable (translated from Arabic):

> "I work in delivering orders **for my own account** and wish to deal with you as a **service provider for order delivery**... I undertake **not to work directly or indirectly with any other delivery company or restaurant outside the scope of your company's work**... this request does not entail any rights or obligations toward you, nor does it grant me authority to represent your company."
Source: https://join-us.beeorder.com/join-us (T&Cs section, live form, accessed 2026-09-03)

This is legally significant on two fronts:
1. **Employment classification:** BeeOrder frames riders as independent service providers/sole proprietors ("لحسابي الخاص"), explicitly disclaiming employer-employee obligations. No mention of social security (تأمينات اجتماعية), paid leave, or labor-law benefits.
2. **Exclusivity clause:** BeeOrder's contract **explicitly forbids multi-apping** — a direct contradiction risk to the "riders often work gig-style across apps" assumption in Bosla's prior research (see §5 below for nuance — this is the *contractual* position, not necessarily the *practiced* reality).

🟡 **LIKELY — no dedicated gig-worker/platform-labor law exists in Syria as of Sept 2026.** Searches for "قانون العمل سوريا عمال منصات توصيل" surfaced only:
- General commentary that Syrian labor law enforcement is weak/informal outside gig work too ("قوانين بلا تطبيق... عمال سوريا خارج مظلة التأمينات," Al Jazeera-linked video, 2026) — indicates broad non-enforcement of labor protections generally, not platform-specific legislation.
- The **ILO adopted a new binding international convention on platform-worker protections** in 2025/2026 (referenced in Al Jazeera video description) covering delivery/ride-hail/freelance workers — but there is **no evidence Syria has signed, ratified, or domesticated it**. This is a global framework, not Syrian law.
- No Syrian Ministry of Labor circular, decree, or draft bill specifically addressing app-based/gig/platform workers was found.

🔴 **UNCONFIRMED / gap:** No evidence of any regulatory registration requirement for gig platforms vis-à-vis their courier workforce (e.g., no requirement to register riders with social security, no minimum-wage applicability confirmed for piece-rate gig work). Treat this as a genuine legal grey zone — Bosla should not assume either full legal cover *or* full legal exposure; get local counsel confirmation before finalizing the courier contract template.

**What this means for Bosla's contract design:** The BeeOrder model (independent contractor + exclusivity clause + refundable equipment deposit + no guaranteed minimum wage, only a minimum-activity floor) is the de facto market template. Bosla can likely replicate this structure without inventing new legal risk, but should not assume it insulates the company from Syria's general labor-law informal-employment scrutiny (per the Al Jazeera clip, this is a live public/media issue in Syria generally, i.e., political/reputational risk even where enforcement is weak).

## 3. Vehicle ownership & financing

🟢 **CONFIRMED — couriers own/bring their own vehicle; platform does not provide it.** BeeOrder's sign-up form asks the applicant to select their vehicle type from a checklist (سيارة / موتور / دراجة كهربائية / دراجة هوائية / مشي — car / motorcycle / e-bike / bicycle / on-foot), meaning the rider supplies the mode of transport. The company only issues a delivery bag/kit and uniform (against the refundable 550,000 SYP deposit) — not a vehicle.
Source: https://join-us.beeorder.com/join-us

🟡 **LIKELY:** This "bring your own vehicle" model is standard across Syrian delivery gig work generally — JobInSyria's driver-jobs guide describes conditions as needing "your own means of transport and a valid license" as baseline requirements before applying, with no mention of any company-provided-vehicle track.
Source: https://jobinsyria.com/delivery-driver-jobs-syria/

🔴 **UNCONFIRMED / notable exception found:** One job post — "كابتن توصيل (الدراجة مؤمنة) — شركة توربو للتوصيل، طرطوس" — advertises "insured bike" as a differentiator, and other postings ("سائق دليفري بدراجة نارية — Wasel-2U") specify the rider must bring a motorcycle. This suggests a spectrum: most operators require the rider to own the bike; a minority (like Turbo Delivery in Tartus) offer some insurance/asset coverage as a recruiting edge. No operator was found that provides the vehicle itself (i.e., no confirmed leasing/company-fleet model in the Syrian coastal delivery market).

🔴 **No motorcycle financing/installment product found specific to Syria.** Search for "تمويل شراء دراجة نارية تقسيط سوريا 2026" returned no Syrian results — only an unrelated Moroccan product (Wafasalaf "Salaf Moto," Morocco-only, not transferable). This is a genuine gap: **there is no confirmed formal moto-financing/leasing scheme in Syria** as of this research. Riders likely acquire bikes via cash purchase, informal family/community lending, or secondhand-market financing outside any documented fintech/bank product. This is a meaningful constraint if Bosla considers a "rider fleet" or "bike-as-a-benefit" onboarding incentive — there's no existing local financial rail to plug into; any such program would likely have to be built/financed in-house or via a local microfinance/informal partner, adding cost and risk.

## 4. Motorcycle/vehicle insurance — update since Sept 2, 2026

🟡 **LIKELY — mandatory third-party liability insurance for vehicles exists in principle, reaffirmed mid-2026, but rider/motorcycle-specific enforcement remains unclear.**

Key finding: **Syria's Ministry of Transport reaffirmed compulsory (إلزامي/إجباري) third-party vehicle insurance via Decision No. 440-1 dated 1 June 2025**, and the state-owned General Syrian Insurance Establishment (المؤسسة العامة السورية للتأمين) has been actively urging citizens to obtain it.
Source: SANA (state media) via Facebook post — https://www.facebook.com/sana.gov/posts/... (headline: "المؤسسة العامة السورية للتأمين تدعو المواطنين إلى الحصول على التأمين... استناداً للقرار الصادر عن وزير النقل رقم 440-1 تاريخ 1-6-2025")

🟡 **LIKELY — a NEW mandatory-insurance tariff/rate schedule takes effect 1 July 2026** ("التعرفة الجديدة للتأمين الإلزامي للمركبات اعتباراً من ١/٧/٢٠٢٦"), per a Threads post dated 17 August 2026 referencing the update. This indicates the mandatory insurance regime is **active, being enforced/updated, and pricing is being revised** — this is new information beyond the Sept 2 baseline (which had found "no confirmed motorcycle liability insurance law"). It appears the *general* mandatory vehicle insurance law does exist and applies broadly, but:

🔴 **UNCONFIRMED — whether/how this applies distinctly to motorcycles/delivery bikes** (as opposed to cars generally), and whether enforcement reaches the informal/unregistered-motorcycle population that dominates delivery gig work. The Dalelo consumer guide ("أسعار التأمين على السيارات في سوريا 2026," dalelo.com) discusses "التأمين الإلزامي" (compulsory insurance covering third-party damages) as a car-insurance category but doesn't confirm motorcycle-specific rates or a segregated moto policy product.

🔴 **No confirmed statement from an insurance company that they specifically underwrite delivery-rider motorcycle liability policies**, and no B2B product found (e.g., no "fleet insurance for gig platforms" offering located). The one competitor signal — "الدراجة مؤمنة" (Turbo Delivery, Tartus) advertising an insured bike as a perk — suggests **individual operators are voluntarily/selectively insuring bikes**, not that this is a legally mandated, universally-enforced baseline.

**Net update vs. prior research:** The mandatory *vehicle* insurance regime is real, statutory, and actively being repriced (per Aug 2026 news), which is new/more concrete information than "no confirmed law" — but it remains **UNCONFIRMED whether motorcycles used for gig delivery are covered, enforced, or priced within this regime in practice**, and reckless-minor-driving complaints (per prior research) suggest low real-world enforcement regardless of the law's existence on paper.

**Recommendation:** Before finalizing G2 risk language, have a Syria-based legal/insurance contact directly query the General Syrian Insurance Establishment (or a licensed insurer) on: (a) whether motorcycles have a distinct compulsory tariff line under the July 2026 schedule, and (b) whether third-party delivery platforms carry any secondary liability exposure if an uninsured/insured rider causes an accident while fulfilling a platform order. This could not be resolved from public search.

## 5. Multi-apping — evidence and competitor exclusivity policies

🟢 **CONFIRMED — at least one major incumbent (BeeOrder) contractually PROHIBITS multi-apping.** Its rider T&Cs state couriers must not "work directly or indirectly with any other delivery company or restaurant outside the scope of [BeeOrder's] work." This is an explicit, binding exclusivity clause in the standard onboarding contract.
Source: https://join-us.beeorder.com/join-us

🟡 **LIKELY — despite the contractual clause, multi-apping is a known/discussed phenomenon in the broader Syrian gig market**, based on:
- JobInSyria's driver guide explicitly advises job-seekers: **"لا تحصر حالك بجهة وحدة... تابع إعلانات الوظائف بشكل يومي"** ("don't limit yourself to one employer... follow job postings daily") — implicitly normalizing working across multiple delivery employers/apps as a livelihood strategy, even if individual platforms forbid it contractually.
- The existence of an explicit anti-multi-apping clause at BeeOrder is itself indirect evidence that the practice is common enough to need contractual prohibition — companies don't write clauses against behavior that isn't happening.
- No direct forum/testimonial evidence (e.g., a rider explicitly stating "I drive for BeeOrder and YallaGo simultaneously") was found in this search pass — this remains **an inference, not a directly witnessed confirmation**. 🔴 Flagged for follow-up via Facebook rider groups (e.g., "مجموعة مندوبين المبيعات في اللاذقية") with direct-message outreach or native-language crowd-sourcing.

🟡 **LIKELY — BeeOrder uses loyalty/retention mechanics rather than pure piece-rate to counter attrition/multi-apping incentives:** its site advertises "0% commission," "حوافز وحسومات" (incentives and discounts) and a "برنامج تكريم دوري" (periodic recognition/rewards program) as retention tools, alongside the minimum-activity floor (21 days/month, 6 hrs/day) which functions as a soft exclusivity mechanism — a rider meeting that floor has little slack to also work full shifts elsewhere.

🟡 **YallaGo (ride-hail + motorcycle delivery)** frames its driver relationship as "Safeers" (informal partners) with "Exclusive Rewards and Incentives," "Passenger Referrals," and testimonials emphasizing "freedom and flexibility" — language consistent with a gig/non-exclusive framing (contrasts with BeeOrder's explicit prohibition), but no explicit exclusivity or anti-multi-apping clause was found in YallaGo's public driver page.
Source: https://yallago.net/drivers

**Implication for Bosla:** Multi-apping risk is real and structurally incentivized by the region's thin per-app income ($100–250/mo), but incumbents already fight it via (a) contractual exclusivity (BeeOrder) and (b) minimum-activity floors + non-cash incentives (both). Bosla should assume couriers will multi-app unless given a strong reason not to (higher effective per-order rate, faster payout cycle, or exclusive access to high-value order flow) — copying BeeOrder's blunt contractual ban without matching its retention incentives is unlikely to work on its own.

## 6. Payment gateway merchant fees — Sham Cash, Syriatel Cash, Paymera, TSA/API

### Sham Cash
🟢 **CONFIRMED — cash-withdrawal fee capped at 0.3% (3 per thousand), announced 14 August 2026.** Sham Cash announced a maximum cash-withdrawal commission of "three per thousand" (0.3%) for converting e-wallet balance to cash via authorized agents, aligned with Central Bank of Syria directives, and opened a formal online complaints channel for overcharging.
Source: SP Today, "Sham Cash Caps Cash-Withdrawal Fee at 0.3%, Opens Complaints Channel," 14 Aug 2026 — https://www.sp-today.com/en/news/sham-cash-withdrawal-fee-cap-2026

🟢 **CONFIRMED — separate, slightly higher published rates for withdrawal vs. deposit:** a second source states Sham Cash commission "can reach up to 3 per thousand (0.3%) for withdrawals and 2 per thousand (0.2%) for deposits."
Source: Jarayid, "Sham Cash details withdrawal and support" — https://jarayid.com/en/syria/1491743/sham-cash-details-withdrawal-and-support

🟡 **These are CASH AGENT/withdrawal fees, not confirmed merchant/POS transaction fees.** No published merchant-facing fee schedule (e.g., % taken per online sale routed through Sham Cash) was found. Sham Cash's own site claims "No Fees... no hidden fees" for standard wallet-to-wallet transfers (shamcash.sy/en), which is inconsistent/ambiguous alongside the 0.2–0.3% agent-cashout fees reported elsewhere — likely means: wallet-to-wallet transfers are free, but converting wallet balance to physical cash via an agent carries the 0.2–0.3% fee. **No e-commerce/merchant integration fee percentage was found.**

🔴 **UNCONFIRMED — Sham Cash's original public-sector payroll rollout (2025) was reported to charge ~0.5% cash-withdrawal commission before the Aug 2026 cap**, per an economist's critique calling for a cap "not exceeding 0.1%, instead of the current 0.5%" (Apr 2025, North Press Agency). This implies fees have been **trending down** (0.5% → 0.2–0.3% by Aug 2026) under regulatory/public pressure — useful context on direction of travel but the historical 0.5% figure is from a secondary commentary, not an official rate card.
Source: https://npasyria.com/en/124295/ (North Press, 12 Apr 2025)

### Syriatel Cash
🟡 **LIKELY — an unofficial third-party API exists (techlite-dev "api-syria-sdk" on GitHub, and a "melchersman.com" API docs page) exposing Syriatel Cash balance/transaction endpoints**, including sample JSON showing a `"fee"` field (shown as `0.0` in the sample transaction) — but this is a **third-party/reseller integration wrapper, not an official Syriatel-published merchant fee schedule.** No confirmed official percentage fee for merchant settlement via Syriatel Cash was found.
Sources: https://api.melchersman.com/syr-cash/api-docs ; https://github.com/techlite-dev/api-syria-sdk
🔴 **Flag:** These look like grey-market/unofficial API wrappers around Syriatel's consumer app (screen-scraping or reverse-engineered), which is itself a legal/reliability red flag if Bosla were to depend on this route for production payments — do NOT treat these as a viable integration path without verifying legitimacy and terms directly with Syriatel.

### Paymera
🟢 **CONFIRMED — Paymera is a real, bank-integrated card payment gateway with an active Visa partnership.** Paymera "develops electronic payment solutions and connects bank cardholders with merchants through a secure, local network," and announced a **strategic partnership with Visa** to enable international card payments in Syria, including a live test transaction with Fransabank Lebanon.
Sources: https://ssf.gov.sy/en/media-center/strategic-partnership-between-paymera-and-visa ; https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/visa-tests-first-live-international-transaction-in-syria (test transaction reported "6 days ago" relative to a recent-2026 crawl)

🟡 **LIKELY — settlement is to a Syrian bank account (confirming Bosla's prior finding on bank-account prerequisite):** the Makook e-commerce guide (24 July 2026) confirms "Paymera is a local payment gateway integrated with Syrian bank accounts... with settlement to a Syrian bank account," and integrates directly into e-commerce dashboards like Makook's.
Source: https://www.makook.app/en/blog/ecommerce-in-syria

🔴 **No numeric merchant fee percentage or settlement timeline (T+N days) was found published anywhere for Paymera.** This confirms the prior research finding: **no self-service published fee schedule exists**; the onboarding/pricing must be obtained directly from Paymera via commercial negotiation, consistent with the previously-found requirement of a physical branch visit + pre-existing Syrian bank account.

### General e-commerce payment landscape (context, not gateway-specific)
🟢 **CONFIRMED — Cash on Delivery (COD) remains "by far the most used payment method" for Syrian online commerce**, per the Makook guide, with card (Paymera), e-wallet (Sham Cash), and bank transfer as secondary/minority rails. Domestic courier companies (Al-Qadmous, Aramex, Wadily, Yalla Go) are described as also handling **"cash against delivery"** collection-and-remittance directly (i.e., some couriers act as informal payment collection agents on behalf of merchants) — relevant precedent for Bosla's own cash-handling/reconciliation design for riders.
Source: https://www.makook.app/en/blog/ecommerce-in-syria (24 July 2026)

**Net finding for G2:** No usable numeric merchant fee schedule was found for Sham Cash-as-merchant-gateway or Paymera. The only hard numeric fee data obtained is Sham Cash's **consumer cash-withdrawal fee cap (0.2–0.3%)**, not a merchant transaction fee — this is a materially different number from what a super-app would pay per transaction and should NOT be used as a proxy for Bosla's payment-processing cost assumptions. Direct commercial outreach to Paymera and Sham Cash's business/enterprise teams remains the only path to a real fee quote, confirming the prior finding that there's no public self-service fee discovery in this market.

## What breaks our current assumptions

1. **Motorcycle insurance is NOT simply "unregulated"** — there is an active, statutory, recently-repriced (1 July 2026) mandatory vehicle insurance regime in Syria (Ministry of Transport Decision 440-1, 1 June 2025). If this is found to apply to motorcycles (still unconfirmed), Bosla's rider onboarding may need a compliance step (proof of insurance) that wasn't previously assumed necessary, and the company could carry secondary liability exposure for uninsured riders fulfilling platform orders — this needs urgent direct legal confirmation before launch, not just background research.

2. **Multi-apping may be actively discouraged by contract, not just informally tolerated** — BeeOrder's exclusivity clause means Bosla's "riders work gig-style across apps" assumption is only half-true: it's true in practice/spirit (informal norm, advised by job-market content) but competitors are actively trying to contractually stop it. Bosla's own rider-acquisition strategy (e.g., poaching BeeOrder riders) may run into these existing exclusivity contracts as a friction point, and Bosla should decide upfront whether to compete on exclusivity (matching BeeOrder) or openness (differentiating like YallaGo appears to).

3. **No motorcycle financing/leasing product exists in Syria** — if Bosla's business model assumed it could offer "get a bike, work for us" as an onboarding lever (common in other markets, e.g., Gojek/Grab-style vehicle financing), **that lever does not exist locally and would have to be built from scratch** (partnering with a bank/microfinance actor or self-financing), which is a bigger lift and cost than assumed.

4. **Real per-app rider income ($100–250/mo) is thin enough that a rival with even a modest per-order premium or faster payout cycle could plausibly pull riders from BeeOrder/YallaGo** — but conversely, Bosla's own unit economics for rider payouts need to clear this bar (or offer superior order density/tips) to be competitive, and this number should directly inform Bosla's per-delivery rider payout modeling in the strategy doc.

5. **Payment gateway fee opacity is confirmed, not resolved** — despite extensive searching, no numeric merchant fee schedule exists publicly for the two most viable card/wallet rails (Paymera, Sham Cash). Bosla's financial model for G2 should treat payment processing cost as a **direct-negotiation unknown requiring in-market outreach**, not extrapolate from the 0.2–0.3% consumer cash-out fee, which is a different transaction type.

6. **BeeOrder's "0% commission from rider income" claim** is notable — if accurate, it implies BeeOrder's revenue model extracts its margin entirely from the merchant/customer side, not from riders. If Bosla planned to fund a rider commission-based margin, it may face a market-education/positioning challenge going up against an incumbent already marketing "we take nothing from you."
